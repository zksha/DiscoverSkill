## Self-Critique via Steel-Manning

After reaching a preliminary answer, actively argue against it.

**How to apply:**
1. State your current best answer
2. Try to construct the strongest possible case for a *different* answer
3. If the counter-argument is weak: proceed with your original answer (confidence boost)
4. If the counter-argument is strong: revise your reasoning

**Steel-manning questions:**
- "What assumption am I making that could be wrong?"
- "Under what circumstances would the opposite be true?"
- "What evidence would change my mind?"
- "Is there a more precise or more general answer I'm ignoring?"

**Why this matters:** Confirmation bias causes us to stop reasoning once we reach *any* answer. Forcing a counter-argument breaks that pattern and surfaces overlooked information.

**Time-box this step:** Spend at most 30% of total reasoning time on critique — it is a check, not a full re-solve.
