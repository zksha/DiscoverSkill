## Analogical Reasoning

When a problem feels novel, search for a familiar analogy.

**How to apply:**
1. Identify the core structure of the problem (ignore surface details)
2. Ask: "Have I seen something structurally similar before?"
3. Map the analogy: "A is to B as X is to Y"
4. Transfer the solution method from the known domain to the new one
5. Adjust for any differences between the analogy and the original

**Good analogy sources:**
- Physical phenomena (waves, pressure, flow) often map to abstract systems
- Geometric relationships often generalize algebraically
- Historical patterns often repeat with different actors

**Caution:** Analogies are guides, not proofs.  Always verify the transferred solution actually holds in the new domain.

**Signals that analogical reasoning helps:**
- You encounter a category you know little about
- The question involves relationships or proportions
- You feel "this reminds me of something"
